Findings · Fishers AI workshop · October 27 to November 3, 2026

What Moved The Room

We rebuilt one presentation, slide by slide, for a week. Every version went in front of the same simulated room of local business owners. Here's what actually changed how many of them bought, what didn't, and why.

The presentation. A free evening AI workshop for local business owners in Fishers, Indiana. Jimmy presents. It teaches through eight beliefs and ends in a three-tier offer: Best $4,997, Better $2,997, Good $997 (the meeting). Buying happens at the back of the room that night.

The test. 50 simulated owners (roofers, salons, pizza shops, dentists, accountants, food trucks), each with a business, a temperament and a money situation. Each version was read twice by fresh readers who never saw another version. They bought or didn't, and said why.

The catch. The same deck, read twice, swings by 3 to 5 buyers. So a change counts here only when it moved more than that, or when the reasons people gave changed with it. These are predictions, not results. The live room on November 9 is the judge.

What Made The Difference

Moved it up

1. Prove The Marketer's Eye, Then Show It At Work

The biggest single gain came from one section. Version 16.2 stopped asking the room to try good, better, best themselves. Instead: here's what a marketer sees. Study one: one option, 10 of 100 buy; a choice, 66 of 100. Study two: add a third option and people spend 43% more. Then: "So now you can see what I saw that Jamon didn't." Then his estimate, before and after.

Sales went from about $42,400 to about $48,400. 14 of the 100 readers named that section as what moved them, and 12 of those 14 bought. Buyers rated "I need someone with a marketer's eyes, not just the tool" 4.3 out of 5. Non-buyers, 2.9.

The sale lives in the gap between the tool and the eye. Borrow outside proof that the eye is real, then show yours at work on a real client.

Moved it up

2. A Number They Believe Beats A Bigger Number

Jim's bread company loses $4.58 on every box. Early versions led with the big finish: about $673,000 a year. It was the most doubted number in the room, named by 17 of 24 owners. Version 14 rebuilt it from the bottom: the math per box, $140 a box as a marketing budget against the other guy's $7, then "cut it by 70% and he's still six times the other guy." The $673,000 dropped to a small P.S.

Same three rooms: 38 buyers became 42. The offer's values were brought into a believable range in the same version, so the two share the credit, but the reasons people gave moved the most on this one: most owners bought right past the P.S.

Lead with the number they can check in their head. Let the big one ride underneath.

Moved it down

3. Translating The Example Made It Worse

The trades owners said Jim's bread felt far from them. So we added a "for you" slide after each of Jim's five money ideas: a maintenance plan for a roofer, a membership for a salon, the shampoo in the chair. It was the right instinct, and it cost about 3 buyers and $5,000.

Why: it made the stretch full of projected numbers longer. More time with "say a quarter of his boxes subscribe" and "$7 an insert" meant more time to doubt them. The owners who named Belief 5 as what held them back went from 6 to 14. With the five slides out, it was 10, and sales came back.

What stayed: one line on the total slide, "For you, that's every order. Every job." It does the translating without the extra minutes.

Never add length to the part of the night built on projections. Translate in a line, not a slide.

Didn't move sales

4. Easier To Follow Isn't The Same As More Sales

Version 16 made every section a clean lesson. It read easier every time, and it sold a little less. Later, a full sweep fixed every hand-off that didn't land, every callback that pointed at nothing, every promise the opening made and never paid off. People who got lost dropped from 32 of 50 to 27. Flow went up. Sales didn't move.

Clarity is the floor, not the lever. Fix it because a confused room can't buy, but don't expect it to sell for you.

Didn't move sales

5. Telling The Truth About Time Cost Nothing

The pizza shop's AI phone agent was pitched as built "in five minutes." Later the night admitted it "takes time and effort to get right." We changed the early claim to "about a day." Sales held: 19.5 before, 20.5 after.

When the honest version is just as strong, there's no reason to keep the exaggeration around for someone to catch.

Moved it up, a little

6. Late In The Night, Show The Work Instead Of Doing It Live

Live AI demos in the second half made the room wait on the machine. Version 16.1 kept one live moment (Jimmy calls the pizza shop and the AI answers) and walked the other builds through screenshots of what was actually done. Sales recovered from 14.5 to 17.5 buyers.

Save live for the one moment that has to be live. Everything else, show the receipts.

What Never Changed

Across every version, the same things held people back:

  • The price against a tight quarter. Most no's happen at the offer, and most of those are about cash, not doubt.
  • Deciding tonight without a spouse or partner.
  • Jim's numbers reading as estimates. "Only one real result shown." The fix is a real number, not more slides.
  • "$14 a day" for what is a lump sum. The analytical owners flag it every time.

We tested two ways around the "not tonight" crowd on version 16. A free 24-hour hold: 11 bought that night plus 22 holds, about 20 buyers and $49,940 after a day, but only if every hold gets a call the next morning. And no push to decide tonight: 7 that night, 15 by week's end, the same money, later. We kept "tonight." A pile of holds in the real world becomes phone chasing that won't close the way a simulation says it will.

How To Use This

  • Test the change, not the comment. Half of what we tried came from what a simulated person said. Some of it helped, some of it hurt. A change stays only if the sales hold it up.
  • Know your noise. Read the same version twice before you believe any difference. Here that's 3 to 5 buyers out of 50.
  • Know when to stop. Once changes stop moving the number beyond the noise, the next lesson comes from a live room, not another pass on the deck.
