My notes from Dan Kennedy

Mind Hijacking

  • The Decoy
    • The Economist's subscription offer
      • Internet only, $59: 18 took it.
      • Print only, $125: 0 took it.
      • Print and internet, $125: 84 took it.
    • Without the print-only option, more people would have just taken the $59 offer.
      • With only internet vs. the combo, 68 took internet only and 32 took the combo (out of 100).
    • Adding an option nobody takes makes the combo the no-duh choice.
    • Decoy for our reverse selling offer
      • Pay in full and you also get 4 personal one-on-ones.
    • The $200 and $400 bread maker
      • The decoy switches the mindset from yes/no to either/or.
      • You should always have at least two options for every offer.
      • A countdown timer on the pre-cart OTO page worked. It didn't work when it was a basic OTO.
  • Make The Unfamiliar Familiar
    • The trash can icon on the Apple desktop.
  • Encouraged Assumption
    • Informational social influence.
    • A guy with a red umbrella walked around, and people started asking if it was going to rain.
    • Chiropractors in a doctor's outfit increased prepay conversions.
  • No Movement Has Ever Started Without A Devil
    • The need for a scapegoat.
    • Us vs. them.
      • For the reverse selling offer, "them" is the people who just teach you to sell better. The closers are "them." So are the time-wasting leads on the phone.
  • Greed
    • 5% of the population will never lie, cheat or steal.
    • 5% will lie and cheat relentlessly.
    • The rest is situational, based on:
      • Perceived need.
      • Whether they can get away with it undetected.
      • Their ability to rationalize the behavior.
  • The Power Of New
    • The best feeling you have is right before you get the thing.
      • Right before, you imagine all the possibilities in it.
      • After you get it, you see it for the reality it is.
      • Me, with all the vapes.
    • For the reverse selling offer: emphasize it as a new and different experience for everyone already selling high ticket.
  • The Hedonic Treadmill
    • We always return to a stable level of happy, but we're constantly chasing more.
  • Comparative Or Positional Status
    • Nobody is into self-improvement. They're into self-improvement compared to other people.
    • When restaurants removed tips, the servers did way worse. People need positional competition.
    • When Weight Watchers took away weekly weigh-ins, its effectiveness went way down.
    • Network marketers' weekly meetings give props for who brought in how many people. That positional status drives action.
    • "Lose more weight than your friends" converted 3.1 times better than "Lose X pounds for the summer."
  • Marketing To The Affluent
    • The more money people have, the more they spend on things that reflect their status.
    • Poor people buy things that represent their aspirational status.
    • Add sunk cost bias to this.
      • Dan and horse racing: with 23 horses, he's already in too deep.
    • Marketing to dentists works through sunk cost bias too, because of the time and effort they've invested.
    • Sunk cost isn't just money.
  • The Optimistic Bias
    • Combine sunk cost and optimistic bias, and you have something powerful.
      • Example: slot machine gambling.
    • Use this to show people how far they've come in effort, time and investment toward their goals.
