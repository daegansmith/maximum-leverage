Buying Happiness

Subject: Buying Happiness.

In the shower this morning, I started thinking about that old saying...

"Money can't buy happiness."

And I wondered...

Why would anyone say or believe something like that?

Now, I don't think money itself produces eternal joy or anything like that.

After all...

Look at the countless tales of despair attached to the winners of those mega-millions lotteries.

Littered among them you'll find greed, gluttony, powerful jealousy, and every color in the rainbow of sin, and they ALL tend to end badly, but...

I do believe that you unequivocally CAN buy happiness.

Let me tell you why...

I remember one time, as an awkward, chunky kid sporting my fresh new high-top fade, walking to assembly about two months into the school year.

The walk was hilly and probably a little short of half a mile.

We held our Monday morning assemblies in the school gym, which was separate from the main school, so we walked.

Rain, sleet or snow... we walked.

About halfway, where it started to get really hilly, I heard Rich, an eighth grader, behind me say...

"Daegan, why are your legs so skinny, but you have boobs like a girl?"

I had no answer. I felt its sting to my core. I just put my head down and kept walking up the hill.

I had little kiddie man boobs, and I carried them with me until the age of... I don't know, let's say 30.

As you can probably imagine, I was always self-conscious about taking my shirt off.

Having boobs and being a boy ain't all it's cracked up to be.

By about 25, I weighed about 235 pounds, had about 7% body fat, and could bench press 430 pounds for three sets of six, and guess what?

I still had those pesky boobs.

God knows I tried to get rid of those bad boys through discipline and sweat. That's why...

When I see all those eBooks claiming they "cure" man boobs, I get pissed.

It's horse shit.

Gynecomastia is a medical condition that can only be solved by one thing.

Surgery.

So I bought myself a present.

You guessed it...

A boob job.

And I can tell you flat out, it was THE best $8,000 I've ever spent.

I am happier.

Example #2:

I don't know...

Call me crazy, but I love buying my grandma stuff.

One Christmas, I bought her a brand new, big-ass LED TV for her and my grandfather to enjoy.

Just imagining them sitting there enjoying it put a big ol' smile on my face.

Buying stuff for people I care about makes me happy.

Now, here's one more reason why I think you CAN, and in fact SHOULD, buy happiness.

You know the story of how I got started online, at that Holiday Inn.

Well...

What I didn't tell you is that as soon as I got home from that Holiday Inn, like a crack fiend, I scoured the internet for my second "fix" of how to make money online.

I came across a book called "Google Cash" by a guy named Chris Carpenter, and I remember reading the sales letter like 11 times.

Why?

All the ideas about posting links online and making $30,000 a month were incredibly seductive. I mean...

Heart racing.

Skin tingling.

"Oh my gosh, I'm on the verge of something HUGE" seductive.

But every time...

I got to the part where it said "now give me $67 and I'll give you this book," I couldn't do it.

I don't know why.

Thinking back, I was SO naive.

I couldn't get my head around spending $67 on some eBook.

"The audacity of this guy Chris to ask me for that kind of money."

Somehow, I finally clenched my jaw, pulled out the credit card, and did the deed.

And...

Just like with heroin, it's NOT the first hit that gets you. It's the second.

An internet addict was born.

Unlike a heroin addiction, though...

This one is about the ONLY purely positive addiction I can think of. But I digress...

It gets better.

So I'm out there buying everything I can get my hands on, making a dollar here and a dollar there, but by no means in profit, and then...

Matt Morris, now the best-selling author of "The Unemployed Millionaire," invites me to my first marketing seminar.

It was right here in Washington, DC.

It was a buddy ticket, but still...

It cost me $800, and I went back and forth on whether it was a smart or dumb use of my dough, right up until I got there.

Being there changed my life, for three reasons I'll get to in a minute.

I remember being SO nervous when I went to pick up Matt at his hotel that first morning.

He flew in from Texas, and being a townie, the least I could do was give him a ride to the big show. Well...

We valet the car, and I immediately start feeling shaky and cold.

So nervous.

"Who am I? I'm not making money. Matt's a millionaire, and all these people I'm seeing... wow, they're the gurus I see online. But who am I? I don't deserve to be here."

Time passes.

We go out to dinner the first night, and I remember sitting there with Tellman Knudson, who had just cracked his first $800,000 year. (I remember because it's ALL he talked about.) And...

A few seats down the table was my now buddy and fellow college wrestler, Russell Brunson. (I guarantee he doesn't remember that dinner at the Capital Grille, but I do. I even remember he got a steak sandwich or something like that.)

He hadn't made eight figures in a year yet, but he was all over the internet, one year older than me, and making millions. And there I am...

Eating dinner with them because I bought an $800 ticket to an event.

So exciting.

Here's what that did to me, and you'll find the same thing happens to you at the first event you go to...

It made the idea of success real.

These people I saw all over the internet were now very real.

Not better than me. Just slightly ahead of me on the curve.

If not for that event and what happened there, my belief that I could do it myself would never have developed.

By the second day, I'm having the time of my life.

Every single presenter was dropping marketing knowledge atomic bombs.

I'm sitting in the front row with Matt in the VIP section (because he's a VIP) and...

I couldn't take notes fast enough.

Then it happened.

A guy from Texas, a little shorter than me, who spoke kinda funny, gets up on stage and starts talking.

My ears perk up like a dog trying to hear something it can't see.

To this day...

I still have the NOTES I took from that presentation, right here on my desk beside me.

They're old, and some of the pages are torn, but man oh man, are those notes a gold mine.

This guy called himself "The King Of Let 'Em Come To You!"

He started by explaining that he'd been a direct response marketer for many years, and...

Just 19 months earlier, he'd started a brand new information marketing business from scratch with...

Literally no money invested, no customers, no lists, no joint venture partners, no merchant account, no nothin'. And in that time, he turned that simple idea into a million-dollar business.

I vividly remember thinking...

"How awesome. What freedom he must feel to command such power. What leverage. 19 months to $1 million a year. Wow."

(Side note: I later beat that record, taking Maximum Leverage from $0 to $128,000 a month in just under six months.)

Naturally, I paid very close attention to everything he said, and it didn't hurt that he said things that made a LOT of sense to me, like...

"I'm in the business of getting and keeping money."

Then he shared that everything he did to create that success was planned before he ever sent a single email.

Next, he laid out a timeline showing exactly where he started and where he was now.

Day 1: First email sent.

Day 18: First check, $700.36.

Day 36: Second check, $2,421.19.

About 21 months in: $87,945.28 in sales.

Then he showed us exactly how he had created his...

"Highly profitable, extremely predictable, completely stable, ultimate lifestyle business."

I bet you're wondering what he shared...

I'll give you a hint:

It had everything to do with what I'm doing right now, with you.

He had me hook, line and sinker.

That presentation flat out changed my life.

I went home and immediately doubled my business.

At the end of his presentation, he offered a full-day consultation for $10,000.

I'm so glad I had the courage to say yes.

That day at the La Quinta in Austin, spending time with someone FAR better than me at what I wanted to master, and eventually would...

"The Fine Art of Money Getting."

It cost me $10,000, and it has made me millions.

He got the short end of the stick (even though I went back the next year and did it again).

Paying for education has bought me a new and better life.

It's bought me freedom.

And freedom has brought me happiness. Now...

You tell me a way to get your man boobs removed without paying a surgeon to do it.

I mean... you can't very well cut yourself open and rip those suckers out yourself.

Tell me you don't love buying gifts for your grandma, and I'll tell you you're a liar.

And you tell me how the heck you're going to learn to make millions online without paying for the privilege of digging into the brain and experience of someone who's already where you want to go.

These are all things I purchased outright.

I bought them.

And when it comes to my education, I'm ALWAYS buying.

I have no ego about it.

Only excitement.

I love helping the economy by spending my cash money on my education, because I learned a long time ago that...

There is no better deal on earth. And...

For one more reason you don't see on the surface...

When you spend large sums of legal tender to better yourself, you're affirming that you're worth it, and you know what?

You are.

To the top,

Daegan
