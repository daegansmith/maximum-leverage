Roy Rogers On River Road

Subject: How To Entertain Through Story

Let's get right to it.

I was going to show you how to create "buying" desire in your emails, but I'm saving that for another day.

Instead, after hanging out at the Hyatt bar last night with the man who first taught me how to really write emails, some 4,000 emails ago, Mr. John Alanis...

I've changed the agenda.

You should be happy he talked me into it.

You're about to see why.

Today, you're learning the magic of...

How to tell stories that entertain.

Before we get to that...

Let me share WHY stories are so badass in my book.

Their power lies in one simple fact. While you're listening to, reading or watching a REALLY good one, you let go of all the...

"I gotta do this... Man, I'm tired... I'm hungry... This is going to be fun... I don't feel like..."

That's going on in your head all day, every day.

At least for a while.

Which is like magic.

Done right, telling the "right" kind of story lets your prospects quite literally sell themselves on your offer.

Think about it like this...

Have you ever been to one of those water parks with the HUGE water slides? Sure, they're fun, but...

You gotta wait in line and climb 20 stories of stairs to get your water slide on once.

What's more fun?

The waiting in line, or the actual sliding?

An offer without the "right" story is like telling your prospects they gotta do all that waiting to go down the slide.

Sure, they'll do it if it means enough to them. But...

An offer WITH the "right" story is like telling them they get to go down a crazy fun water slide, and their prize is waiting at the bottom.

If I have a choice, I'm taking the water slide every time.

Which reveals a hidden benefit of knowing how to tell the "right" story before you reveal your offer.

Your prospects will be excited to buy from you any time you put something new out, because they'll LOOK FORWARD to being entertained by the stories while you sell them your stuff.

(That's a big one. You might want to write it down.)

With the "right" story, you can entertain, build trust, get their full and undivided attention, stoke their desires, and a lot more.

Today, we're focusing on the entertainment part.

If I'm going to show you how to entertain with stories, I'd better share one darn good example, so...

In honor of my mentor Mr. John Alanis's favorite bar story of mine, here's something that happened to me as a high school kid that I don't think...

Has happened to many other people.

Enjoy THE presentation of...

"It Happened At 6:45 a.m., Sunday Morning, At Roy Rogers On River Road."

If I remember right, it was a Saturday night, and...

I was feeling pretty good.

We had just had our high school conference wrestling tournament, and not only did I win, I was named the conference MVP that year.

A far cry from the 5-foot, 198-pound sixth grader with a record of 2 wins and 12 devastating defeats.

I was proud of myself, you know?

I walked out of that tournament, trophy in hand, and my phone rang.

It was my best buddy, Eddie.

He said...

"Congrats, man. I'm proud of you. I know how much that win means to you.

Let's go out and celebrate.

I'm meeting up with Pete at his place in an hour. Come meet us."

To which I replied...

"See you there."

Pete lived off River Road, on a windy road.

I was driving my father's old-school burgundy Lincoln Town Car, and I always loved taking those curves.

I got there in the blink of an eye.

Ed and Pete were in the basement watching TV and having a couple of beers.

I should have known then that something was different about that night.

It was the look in Pete's eye, and the fact that he was SO enamored with his new ball and chain.

(Looking back on my life, so far at least, I don't think I've ever known anyone other than Pete to own a ball and chain.)

See, you gotta understand...

Pete was a friend, but if he were a dog, you'd have to describe him as an extremely territorial pit bull.

He stood all of 6 feet 8 inches tall, and if you didn't know the guy, a lot of times that wasn't good news for you.

Generally, our job, Ed's and mine, was to keep Pete in line when we went out.

I don't know how I got stuck driving, but I did.

Probably because I hadn't had a drink.

We all pile into the "stinkin'" Lincoln and go for a ride.

We arrive at the first party, and within five minutes...

Yep.

You guessed it.

Pete's in a fight.

We yank him away and get out of there fast.

I should have known then that we were in for a LONG night.

What I didn't know was...

What would happen the next morning.

I'll let you fill in the details yourself of what three 17-year-old kids did that night, but I'll tell you for a fact, it WAS fun.

Our celebration came to an end with the new Sunday sun.

By then, I found myself back on River Road, heading back to Pete's place.

Pete, at this point, was VERY drunk.

He sat in the back of the car screaming like a loon.

"I'm hungry!"

"I'M HUNGRYYYYYYYYYY!"

And when a Pete screams for food on a Sunday morning, you know what you do?

You stop at Roy Rogers and you feed him.

It's not there anymore, but back then there was a Roy Rogers right off River Road, and we had to be the first people to pull in.

Like a caged animal set free, we released Pete into the wild.

I still, to this day, feel bad for doing it.

He ran from the burgundy Town Car, hands in the air, screaming, into Roy Rogers.

Immediately after...

The cleaning ladies came running out.

I said:

"Ed, we've got to get out of here. We gotta leave Pete."

Ed replied:

"No, we can't. Give him one more minute."

For whatever insane reason, I complied.

When he came out a minute later with his two bags of Roy Rogers breakfast, I quietly let out a sigh of relief.

But then it happened.

Let me ask you...

Who's up at 6:45 a.m. on a Sunday?

Only two kinds of people.

Loony kids who should have gone to bed LONG ago, and senior citizens on their way to church, and...

You know what they do before church?

They go to Roy Rogers for breakfast.

An old white Cadillac with three senior citizens pulls into Roy Rogers at exactly the wrong moment.

The exact moment this screaming maniac decides to walk out.

Ed and I both look at the car full of seniors and say...

"NO! Don't look at him. PLEASE... don't look him in the eyes."

They couldn't hear us.

It was too late.

It felt like watching a lion stalk its prey and go in for the kill.

They DID look at Pete.

And that's all it took.

It was over.

He saw their gaze, dropped both bags of breakfast, and pulled out the ball and chain.

Imagine it. A 6-foot-8, football-lineman-sized man, ball and chain twirling above his head, running full speed at a Cadillac full of senior citizens on their way to church.

Now...

Imagine how they felt as this crazed animal came at their car.

I felt so sorry for them that morning.

Three strikes.

Bang!

Bang!

Bang!

And that's all it took.

Their windshield. Gone.

My only thought...

"I'm going to jail."

I pulled out of that parking lot.

Pete tried to chase down our car, and that's the last I saw of Pete.

The lesson I learned that night has paid me dividends ever since...

Who you associate with matters a LOT.

I heard years later that Pete had done several stints in jail.

Me?

I study.

I go to seminars, just like the one I'm at right now, and I ONLY associate with good people.

I thank Pete for that lesson.

Who knows where I might have ended up without it.

End of story.

Now, let me ask you...

As you read that story, did your mind drift away from your own thoughts for just a little while?

That's the power of story.

Here's your assignment. Write an email and tell a story.

Extra credit if you can do what I did above, and pull a meaningful lesson out of your story.

To the top,

Daegan
