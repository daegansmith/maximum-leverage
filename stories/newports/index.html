Newports And Red Bull For Breakfast

Subject: Newports And Red Bull For Breakfast.

I bet you have no idea what the title has to do with today's lesson.

Am I right?

Well...

Take a deep breath, because it will all make sense soon enough.

There's one thing that's MORE important than anything else in today's online market, and...

If you know how to attack this one thing the right way (few do), your emails will become FAR more profitable.

Let's talk about the final gatekeeper of people's buying decisions.

Trust.

Imagine getting your prospect all hot and bothered to get your stuff or work with you, and then they ask this question...

"Can I trust this person?"

If they can't answer that with an absolute, unwavering yes, you lose, and they don't buy.

TRUST is the biggest point of leverage most people miss in their email marketing, and because of it, they lose a LOT of sales.

So that's our focus today.

Let's dig in.

How do you create trust?

Well, if you send an email every day, you're already doing it great justice.

That kind of consistency is rare. Just like you never question whether the ground will be there when you step out of bed, because it always has been, when you're consistently there for your prospects, they tend to trust you more.

So...

Every email you send puts coins in the trust bank, even when you don't know it.

There are several more ways to turn up the trust factor in your emails, but since we're in storytelling mode, let's focus on a powerful way to use stories to build extreme rapport.

You know...

Us humans are funny creatures.

We all want "good" stuff to happen to us, but the flat truth is, we pay FAR more attention to potential negatives than to potential positives.

That's why so few people are willing to take small risks that could change their lives, like, oh, I don't know... risking $97 to join Maximum Leverage.

It's silly, but it's true.

It's also why...

One of my best email subject lines for years was "you've been scammed."

People pay attention to bad news, and they REALLY pay attention to bad news told as a story.

Have you ever noticed that all the REALLY good stories have a deep, dark chasm where you wonder how the main character will ever get out?

I'm sure you're nodding your head yes, but have you noticed this...

It's usually right around then that we, the audience, do two things.

1. Pay closer attention than at any other point.

2. Start to really "root" for the main character.

Shared pain is the magical elixir of trust.

So I'm going to show you how to turn all the bad stuff that's happened to you while trying to get your business off the ground into pure TRUST gold.

Sound like a plan?

Let me share a short story first, so you can "feel" how this works. Then I'll show you how to do it yourself.

"Newports And Red Bull For Breakfast." By Daegan Smith

I had just moved to Hawaii on a complete whim.

I got a house with an elevator and a saltwater pool.

It was about a five-minute walk from the beach, and for a marketing geek like me, just knowing...

Direct response legend Joe Sugarman lived right down the road left me speechless.

It was almost too good to believe.

The dream does come true.

All those long, hard years of working, failing, taking insane financial risks...

All the sacrifices, like my girlfriend of four years leaving me because, she said...

"You're not paying enough attention to me."

And...

Having to cut my own father out of my life for a while. It was all worth it.

There I was. In just five months, I had taken my business from complete scratch to over $128,000 a month AND was living the dream in a tropical paradise.

And just when I thought it couldn't get any better...

Within our first two weeks in paradise, we hit an $80,000 week.

Our best week in five months.

Can you imagine how amazing that felt?

Well, life is funny...

Right after that $80k week, I left my favorite Starbucks by the beach and headed home feeling on top of the world.

I got home, pulled into the garage, went for my afternoon stint in the hammock by the pool, and decided to check my email on my iPad.

And that's when I saw it.

News that led me to a place I thought I'd never be in my entire life.

Have you ever woken up in a state of complete panic?

Full of fear and anxiety.

That's exactly how I woke up every day for the next six months.

Hopeless, panicked, and full of anxiety.

My days became a routine of simply trying to manage that anxiety.

I'd wake up at 7 a.m., heart racing, breathing shallow.

It was like waking up into a nightmare.

The only thought in my mind was...

"How do I get out of this?"

I'd sneak off in the car before Kim noticed and head to the convenience store for my medicine.

A steady diet of Newports and Red Bull.

By the second month, I was up to a full pack a day.

I'd head out, pick up a pack, smoke one, drink some Red Bull, and, still panicked, go through the routine over and over again.

By 8:30 a.m., I had done nothing except stand in a parking lot and smoke half a pack of Newports.

At night, I'd keep it going at home, except I'd switch the Red Bull for Foster's and pizza.

Can you imagine? Living in paradise, watching whales, killing your lungs, in a depression so deep you couldn't see the light of day.

Never in a thousand years would I have thought that could be me.

I was only a month or two from my business going bankrupt.

I had about $30,000 in the business account and my costs were about $25,000 a month, so next month, it was game over.

And you know what...

If it weren't for a great friend of mine, I never would have made it through that next month. But because of his advice, slowly...

I was able to break even.

There was life in my business again, confidence was restored, and we started to eke out a small profit.

It wasn't much, but it was something.

Things were looking up.

I decided maybe Maui wasn't the right place for me, and headed back to Washington, DC.

When I got home, I checked my mail and got THE most devastating news of my entire life.

A letter from the IRS, saying I owed them over a million dollars.

From bad to worse.

My financial destruction was imminent.

Can you imagine that happening to you?

You just get yourself back on your feet, and then you get news like this.

What would you have done?

I was totally and completely alone.

And right there, I heard the voice of one of my mentors, John, say:

"The money is in the list and the stories you tell them..."

In that split second, I vowed that no matter what happened to me, no matter what life threw my way, I would never quit doing what I love. And I loved, and still love, telling stories with email...

I put my blinders on, clenched my teeth, and got to work.

One year later, things were FAR better than they'd ever been.

Why?

Two reasons...

1. Email marketing. I went back and did only the things that worked with email, and left all the rest out. If it weren't for email marketing and having a list, I'd have gone bankrupt.

2. My mentors. Over the years, I paid many people far smarter than me to help me think the way they did about marketing. Sure, my business got better, but where that advice was priceless was when the going got tough. Giving a good mentor money will pay you dividends for the rest of your life.

End of story...

So here's what you want to do.

In good stories, there's usually a point where things seem to be going well, and then something happens that pulls the rug out from under the main character.

The character gets through the hardship and gets back on the right path, and then gets hit with something that seems insurmountable.

At that moment, the character has to make a choice: give up, or make a vow.

The hero always makes the vow, out of a definiteness of purpose, and it's based on past experience, usually a mentor.

Share a story in this format, and your audience will root for you and your cause without even knowing it.

With that "rooting," they not only trust you, they need to know how your story ends.

Your homework: write a "trust" story like the one above, and send it to your list.

To the top,

Daegan
