# The Three-Belief Chain

**Status:** Untested · **First written:** October 19, 2026

## The idea

If you can hold an audience's attention, through story, explanation, or plain entertainment, you've earned the right to walk them down a road.

The road is a logical chain of beliefs.

Each belief sets up the next one. By the time they reach the end, the conclusion isn't a pitch. It's inevitable.

I've built chains like that for years. I never put a limit on how many steps.

Then I ran into Perry Belcher's rule: no more than three beliefs. Go past three, and the offer fails.

Put the two together and something clicks. If every belief a person needs to reach "I need you" can be condensed into three that lead to the big one, the road gets shorter and the conclusion lands harder.

That's the idea. I haven't tested it yet.

## Where it came from

- **Rich Schefren.** To sell well, use every tool at your disposal. If you're good enough to keep an audience's attention, a logical chain of beliefs walks them to a conclusion that feels inevitable.
- **Perry Belcher.** No more than three beliefs.

## What I'd test

- The same offer, sold two ways: the full chain, every step I'd normally use, against the chain condensed to three.
- Which one converts.
- The hard part is the condensing: getting to three without dropping a belief the buyer actually needs.
