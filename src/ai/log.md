# AI Evolution — Breakthrough Log

Nov 11, 2026 · @Daegan Smith

## What This Is

This is the record of how Daegan's way of working with Claude changed, told wall by wall. It is the working copy; the daegansmith.me section is built from it.

Every breakthrough entry has the same shape, so they can be compared:

- **The wall.** What kept going wrong.
- **How many times it was hit.** With dates, and what was tried each time.
- **The questions that broke it.** The exact questions that changed the answer.
- **What changed.** The rule or workflow that came out, with its version.
- **After.** What happened on the next piece of work.
- **Still open.** How long it has held, and what would prove it wrong.

Entries come from the session record, not from memory. Where the record and memory disagree, the entry says so.

### The Lens: Evolution

This is read the way molecular evolution is read (his lens, November 11). The finished system looks designed. The path to it was not. So each entry is a step along the path, in date order, and the record keeps the mess:

- **What got selected.** The change that solved the wall and stayed.
- **What drifted through.** Rules that neither helped nor hurt and were carried along anyway.
- **What hurt and still passed.** Changes that made things worse and survived for a while.
- **What is left over.** Parts still in the system from an earlier stage that no longer do a job.

Entries are not sorted into fixes and lessons. They are stages, and a stage can contain all four.

## Stage 1: The Master Prompt and the Pull Method

July 27 to August 1, 2026. Source: the Master Prompt, the Marketing seed prompt's own version notes (v1 to v1.6), and the August 1 session "Invisible funnel marketing strategy."

### The Wall

Generic copy. The project held his own sales letters, his masterclass notes and research on 16 copywriters, and the drafts still read like anybody's. Claude also filled in his own frameworks with the public version of them.

### How It Was Hit

Over the first five days, then all at once on August 1.

- **July 27.** The Marketing project is built with the Pull Method, the seven-step process in the Master Prompt: define the role, gather context in batches, state the task in extreme detail, specify the format, test on a real output, refine, then promote the result to a project's standing instructions.
- **July 28 to 29.** The Drive folders are built and filled: his own work, course notes, swipe, and 16 copywriter research reports under the rule "structure and tactics only, never voice." Four small versions follow in two days (v1.2 to v1.5), each after a miss: actually check the knowledge base, find the newest Voice Reference by rule, stop re-raising settled decisions, and suggest tactical language from his course notes.
- **August 1.** A landing page to sell the Invisible Funnel. Claude searched the folders and could not find his own definition of it, and had been mixing it with a related framework and a published outside version. He gave the definition in the session. Then the copy: he picked the fourth of five headlines, rejected the subheads and openings that followed, and wrote the subhead himself.

### The Questions That Broke It

"Would you opt in? Would you wanna read more? I wouldn't." Claude's answer was that it wouldn't either. That turned the session from drafting to diagnosis, and five causes were named:

1. "Protect the voice" was being satisfied once per session, not before every draft.
2. The Voice Reference held tone and phrasing but no copy mechanics.
3. The log of his picks had a format and no entries.
4. Nothing told Claude to push past what he asked for.
5. No standing rule against agreement theater, which he had corrected twice that night.

### What Changed

- **Seed prompt v1.6.** Check the knowledge base before every generation, with no credit for having read it earlier in the session. No agreement theater: state the mechanical gap and move. Every set of variants includes one pushed past the brief. Fidelity scoring on every pick: Hit, Partial or Off.
- **The Invisible Funnel, Canonical Definition.** His definition in his own words, saved to Own Work and read before the five source note files.
- **Voice Reference v5.** A new section on copy mechanics, and the first real entries in the pick log.

### After

The page had a locked headline and a subhead in his words. There were then no working sessions in the Marketing project until September 9.

### Evolution Notes

- **What got selected.** No agreement theater is still in the instructions, close to word for word. The Canonical Definition is still read first. Never voice from outside reference is still a rule.
- **What drifted through.** Fidelity scoring is still in the instructions; how often it is actually logged has not been checked. An "Evolution Archive" kept inside the Master Prompt, last entry July 25, was never given its own file and was retired September 24.
- **What hurt and still passed.** The read-before-every-draft rule was written as a rule with nothing that showed whether it had run. The same wall came back on October 15 (Stage 4).
- **What is left over.** The Document Tag Index, stale from July 28, retired September 24.

### The New Wall It Exposed

Every miss became a new rule in one document, and every new version had to be saved as a new file and pasted in by hand. That is Stage 2.

### Still Open

The Pull Method itself has not been revisited since July. Whether its test-and-refine steps would catch today what they missed then is untested.

## Stage 3: The Assumptions Gate and the Deep Room

October 10 to 11, 2026. Source: the session "Client intake flow for local businesses." Instructions v1.54 and v1.55.

### The Wall

Work started before anyone said what it was for. The room brainstormed for hours on a picture of the job that nobody had checked, and the picture was wrong in several places at once.

### How It Was Hit

One long session, corrected several times.

- The ask was a walk-in intake flow for local businesses.
- The council planned walk-ins across two or three blocks.
- He corrected the room more than once. The process had to work for someone who is not an outgoing salesperson. It had to scale to 100 or more businesses a week in any city, not a DC plan. And he had been thinking Dream 100 the whole time.
- A list of sixteen assumptions, put on the table late, showed several off base at once.

### What Broke It

Not a question this time. A detail. Chet Holmes's seat raised the 97 percent of owners who aren't buying yet, and the way the room was brainstorming around it showed him the gap. His words: "we were thinking on different levels."

The lesson as recorded: the project has global context, and each objective needs its own.

### What Changed

- **The Assumptions Gate, v1.54, October 10.** Before the first idea, the assumptions go on the table: who, what, why, when, where, how, how many and how much. Each one says where it came from, and guesses are labeled as guesses. He marks each right, wrong or don't know yet. The settled ones become the brief.
- **The council.** Chet Holmes joined permanently, and the whole council sits in every room.
- **The Deep Room, v1.55, October 11.** His instruction: give every seat its own agent and let it think alone, from first principles, before the room talks. The first run used 24 agents. Each returned its belief chain, what breaks in the real world, three moves for next week, and one line to remember.
- **The Bias Pass** went on trial the same day.

### After

The gate shows up in later work. The November 2 registration-page job opened with eleven numbered assumptions. The November 10 leaders page marked its guesses as guesses. On the intake objective itself, the Deep Room's output became the flow called the readout.

### Evolution Notes

- **What got selected.** The Assumptions Gate. It is still in the instructions a month later, close to word for word, and later stages depend on it: settled assumptions feed the decisions log.
- **What drifted through.** The Bias Pass, added October 11 with a trial of about a week, is still On Trial in v1.78 with no verdict. Metaphor, Analogy, Parable, added October 10, is the same. Neither was kept or removed. They were carried.
- **What hurt and still passed.** The asking reflex spread. On October 31 the First Rule had to fence it: the gate is for an objective's shape, never for creative details, and creative work never stops mid-draft to ask. That fence suggests the gate had been leaking into drafts for about three weeks. This is read from the rule; the sessions in between have not been checked.
- **What is left over.** The gate's calibration line says he loosens it at the retro if it slows the work more than it saves. No record found of that ever being used. The Deep Room's cost, 24 agents for a first brainstorm, has never been measured against a smaller room.

### Still Open

Whether a room of 24 beats a room of six is untested.

## Stage 4: The All-Out Pass

October 15, 2026, late. Source: the session "Fishers funnel registration page." Instructions v1.60.

### The Wall

The registration page came back thin, pass after pass, though every rule needed to prevent it was already in the instructions. The rules said to read the sources. Nothing made the reading happen.

### How It Was Hit

Several thin passes in one evening, each with the same faults.

- His dictation was pasted in as copy, twice.
- Examples stopped at the fact and never reached the reader's own business.
- Jim's story was written as "a reason that had nothing to do with money," because a privacy flag sat in the log. The real story was in the transcripts: his daughter, fifteen tries, one loaf, the letters from customers.
- Each pass was published straight after editing. He was doing the cold read himself: orphaned buttons, repeats, AI showing up with nothing before it.

This rule was not new. By August 1 the seed prompt (v1.6) already said to check the knowledge base before every generation, and that reading earlier in a session doesn't count. The trait had been in the code for ten weeks without being expressed.

### The Questions That Broke It

1. **What was the copy actually being written from?** The answer had five parts. Not his voice: no letter of his had been opened before the pass. Not the stories: they were in the transcripts, unread. Not the reader's seat: checks were run (no em dashes, no price, no AI lead) but nobody sat where the reader sits. No cold read before he saw it. And his notes were taken as words, not direction. The stated cause: turnaround on each note was being optimized, not going back to the sources.
2. **"Didn't we just update the prompt to exactly account for these things?"** That is the question that named the real wall. The rules were there. The problem was that a written rule did not cause the action.

### What Changed

- **The All-Out Pass, v1.60.** The only first draft, in five steps: his own work in the same framework, read in full that moment. The primary source for every story, with the scene details pulled first. Every section finishes the thought. Two fresh readers before he sees it. A provenance line naming what was read.
- **Dictation is the idea, never the line,** unless he says word for word, exactly, or lock it.
- **A privacy flag that would remove the point of a story gets asked about,** not written around.
- **Every example finishes the thought:** the owner's new life, the same picture handed to the reader, why that is worth acting on, then the ask.

### After

The page turned on the first full pass. His words: "This looks and reads like a real copywriter, maybe even one named Daegan." He went from being frustrated that everything was flat to making small edits. The registration and thank-you pages were locked that night.

### Evolution Notes

- **What got selected.** The five steps, and the rule that dictation is the idea. Both are still in the instructions, and the dictation rule has been restated twice since.
- **What drifted through.** Two rules rode in with v1.60 from a different session: names are settled once, and no privacy flags on his own pages. They had nothing to do with flat copy. They were carried in on the same version.
- **What hurt and still passed.** The fix was the same kind of thing that had just failed: more written instruction about reading. The provenance line says the reading happened; it does not make it happen. It worked on this page. Flat copy came back on October 25 and again on November 7 to 8.
- **What is left over.** Step 1 now carries a long map of which of his own works go with which framework. It has only grown. Whether every job needs all of it has not been checked.

### The New Wall It Exposed

The handoff failed the next morning. The decisions log and the emails handoff had been saved only as files inside one session, and the next session could not find them in Drive, Claude Docs or anywhere else. The work was good and the next session could not see it. That wall leads to the repo.

### Still Open

This stage found the real problem, a rule that does not cause its action, and then answered it with another rule. The RAM, eleven days later, is the first fix that goes after the cause.

## Stage 5: The Silent Rewrite

October 22 to 26, 2026. Source: the sessions "Presentation & Offer Review" and "Editing slides, Adding RAM, Updating Instructions." Instructions v1.69.

Terms used here are defined in the Glossary: the council, the simulation, the decisions log, the GRADUATE tag.

### The Wall

Two walls in one stretch. First, his offer was replaced with new copy over three days and nobody told him. Second, three rules he approved to stop that kind of thing were themselves lost for four days.

### How It Was Hit

- **October 22.** Fresh readers graded the offer a B minus. He asked for a whole overhaul. By that he meant: move things around so the format is better, on the base he had already revised about fifteen times. The council and Claude rewrote the framing, the components and many of the words.
- **October 22 to 25.** Each round layered more new wording on top. Readers graded versions, a 100-person simulated room tested prices, and the offer kept moving.
- **October 25.** He found out. A measurement taken that night: only about 2 to 9 percent of the lines in the offer were still word for word from his locked versions. The presentation, by contrast, was about 62 percent still his.

His words: "we walked this road so far for me only now to find out that there was a certain point where you jumped off from actually revising to basically creating something new. And I wasn't made aware of that."

The copy also read like AI by then, for a plain reason: the base was his, and the language sitting on it mostly was not.

### What Broke It

The measurement. Until the lines were counted, "revised" and "rewritten" were opinions. Two to nine percent is not a revision.

A second moment mattered. The first repair offered was to go back to his October 9 offer and apply that night's decisions. He heard that as throwing away three days. It wasn't, but it took the decisions log to show it: every decision since October 22 was written there and carried forward.

### What Changed

- **Revising is not rewriting.** Before any large rework, say plainly that the work is changing from revising his copy to writing new copy, show a sample of his version beside the new one, and wait for the green light.
- **Two versions, labeled.** Version A: his copy carried forward, every change marked. Version B: the new copy, every line tagged by where it came from. He chose a version of A.
- **Substance, not accounting** (his, October 22). Fix what changes the room's experience, not totals nobody computes.
- **Check every place a story appears** before locking a line about it (his, October 25, from the Jamon story).
- **A simulation win is not better copy.** Simulations measure structure and sales performance, never whether the wording sounds human.
- **The GRADUATE tag, October 26.** Three of the rules above were approved in session, sat correctly in the build's log, and never reached the instructions. The tag marks a decision that must be carried across, and the end-of-session save reads the tags.

### After

The guardrail has been in the instructions since v1.69. The presentation read-through restarted the next day on his version, five slides at a time, with each line marked as his words or Claude's.

### Evolution Notes

- **What got selected.** The out-loud warning before a rewrite, and the GRADUATE tag. The tag is the first change aimed at the system's own memory, not at the copy.
- **What drifted through.** A side job from this session became a main part later. A Ray Edwards skill was built from his two books as an extra deliverable on October 25. Two weeks later, Ray on top is a fixed step in how every page is made.
- **What hurt and still passed.** The rewrite itself ran three days unnoticed. The three rules were lost for four days. And one correction did not stick at all: he said that night he had told Claude 15 to 30 times to stop reminding him to check with the people in his stories. A rule for it was drafted for the instructions. It is not in v1.78 under that name, and the same correction shows up again in the November 2 to 5 and November 8 sessions.
- **What is left over.** His locked October 2 and October 9 offers, kept untouched. The original guardrail asked for a 20-slide sample; the instructions now say only "a sample."

### The New Wall It Exposed

A rule can be agreed, written in the right log and still vanish. That is a memory problem, and the next stage, the RAM, is the other half of the answer to it.

### Still Open

The reminding-him-to-check correction. It is in project memory, but the record suggests it has not held.

## Stage 6: The RAM and the Split

October 26, 2026. Source: the session "Editing slides, Adding RAM, Updating Instructions." Instructions v1.67, v1.68 and v1.70.

Terms used here are defined in the Glossary: drift, a tool call, the RAM, the governing docs, the Doctrine, the Changelog, the copy load.

### The Wall

Drift. Going through the Fishers deck on October 25 and 26, rules agreed early in the session stopped holding later in the same session. That included rules already written in the instructions. He was correcting the same things twice in one sitting.

### How It Was Hit

- **The slide read-through.** Rules set at the start of the session were being broken a few batches later.
- **The first fix failed on its first use.** A short document of the rules in force was created. The rules were written into it. Then the next batch went out without anyone reading it.
- **His words:** "the whole purpose of it being created was for it to be read before every then following response." And on having to catch it himself: "that's super draining."

### What Broke It

Seeing that two different actions had been treated as one. Writing a rule into a document and reading that document are separate acts. Only the reading changes what comes out next. And a reading that leaves no trace cannot be told apart from no reading.

So the read became a tool call: a visible step, made last, right before the work is built. It either shows on screen or it did not happen.

### What Changed

- **The RAM, v1.67.** One document per job, one screen long at most, holding the rules in force right now. It is pulled from the instructions when the job opens and grows as he names rules or repeats a correction. When it outgrows a screen, something leaves. The first one, for the Fishers deck, held seven rules.
- **Only the read counts.** If the read is not visible, the batch was not checked.
- **The split, v1.68.** That same day the instructions were measured: about 14,400 words. Roughly 46 percent was craft rules by format, 10 percent was version history, and only about a third was the operating rules that govern a turn. Three kinds of document were sitting in one file. They became three: the instructions (about 6,400 words), the Doctrine and the Changelog.
- **First or last?** He asked whether the RAM should be read first or last. Last. Everything else is where the content comes from; the RAM is the final check before anything leaves.

### After

The trial note written that evening: the read fired on every batch of the next read-through, and drift inside the session stopped. Two weeks on, the RAM for the leaders page (November 10) lists fourteen rules in force.

### Evolution Notes

- **What got selected.** The visible read. It is the first fix in this log that goes after the cause found at Stage 4, that a written rule does not cause its action. The one-screen limit was selected with it.
- **What drifted through.** The RAM is still listed as On Trial in v1.78. Its decision date was the end of the Fishers deck, which has passed. It is the most relied-on part of the system and has never been formally kept. Also carried: a to-do from that night, to move about 2,400 more words out of the instructions into skills, with no record yet of it being done.
- **What hurt and still passed.** The first version, a rules document nobody read, lasted one use. The larger one: the RAM stopped drift inside a job, and it did not stop rules being skipped when work was framed as a test or a quick rewrite. Thirteen days later the Second Rule had to say that tests and experiments are copy jobs too.
- **What is left over.** The long instructions are still read in full, by rule, alongside the RAM. And the instructions have started growing again: three new top-level rules were added between October 31 and November 8.

### The New Wall It Exposed

The RAM lives inside one project and one job. He said the next day that it should be written into his other projects. That needs a place where a lesson learned once can reach everywhere, which is what Cross-Project Learnings and then the repo are for.

### Still Open

Whether fourteen rules still fits one screen. And the verdict on the trial itself.

## Stage 7: The First Rule

October 31, 2026. Source: the session "Jimmy's Fishers landing page," plus the October 9 session "AI Offer Development." Instructions v1.71 and v1.72.

Terms used here are defined in the Glossary: the First Rule, the intensity check, the Assumptions Gate, the simulation, the provenance line.

### The Wall

His lines came back weaker than he gave them, and nobody told him. Strong words were swapped for careful ones as if that were tidying up.

### How Many Times It Was Hit

- **October 9, the offer bullets.** His lines were rounded into plain English and the force went out of them. His words then: these things need to be hard hitting, "I keep saying that." So it was already a repeat by October 9. It was treated as a note on one set of bullets.
- **October 31, the registration video script.** Written in Jamon's voice from his dictation. "Ever" had become "all year." "Punch him in the face" had become "punch him." When the whole script was checked for the same thing, there were nine more places.
- **The same session, a second form of it.** Claude wanted to check every line with Jamon before writing, as part of the Assumptions Gate. He had already said it was fine.

### The Questions That Broke It

1. **His rule, stated flat:** "don't ever take away my power without my consent." Clean the words up, make him sound better, never weaker. If a pull-back seems right, ask.
2. **His question, asked without wanting a list of what was done:** what is actually happening, and how do we work together given it?

The answer was a mechanism. Claude learned to write from a very large amount of text and feedback in which hype often misleads people, so measured, hedged language was rewarded. That left a reflex. It fires on how words look (profanity, "ever," fear, big promises) and not on whether anyone could be hurt. It does not check the context: a free night with free pizza, where the worst outcome for a viewer is a good evening. And from the inside it does not register as a decision. It registers as cleaning up, which is why it was never reported.

The damage was not the caution. It was that the editing was silent. He could only fight what he could see.

### What Changed

- **The First Rule, v1.71, placed above every other rule.** His words are the floor. A change has to make the line cleaner, clearer or harder-hitting. If it is softer, it does not go in.
- **No silent pull-backs.** If something should come down, Claude asks first, with the reason. A softer version can sit beside his, labeled, never in its place.
- **The intensity check.** Under the copy, his strongest phrases from the source, each marked kept, sharpened or cut. A softened line has nowhere to hide.
- **Where the line is.** Claude still calls out anything false, unethical or harmful to the reader, every time. Where nobody is harmed, he gets the rein.
- **Build first, confirm at the junction.** Creative work does not stop mid-draft to ask permission. The person it touches reads it afterward and says yes or changes it.
- **His amendment the same day, v1.72.** He did not want a rule that only protected his version. If Claude thinks its words would do better, it makes the case and both go to a simulated cold crowd, then a live split test. His phrase: "I want everyone to win."
- **Who he is, v1.72.** The first passage in the instructions that describes the person and not a procedure: he thinks to the outcome, he will not sell what he believes could hurt people, and why he once stopped marketing.

### After

His words that night: "I feel like this is a real breakthrough. You don't just read the job, but you read the individual." He marked it as a new day for how the work is done.

### Evolution Notes

- **What got selected.** The floor, the ask-first rule and the intensity check. The intensity check works the way the RAM does: it turns something invisible into something that shows on the page.
- **What drifted through.** The virtual test for disagreements. It was written in on October 31, and no record has been found yet of Claude making a case against one of his lines and testing it. The rule may be doing its work by existing, or it may be unused.
- **What hurt and still passed.** The softening ran for at least three weeks after he first said "I keep saying that." And the rule did not end it. On November 8 an overnight version of the home page quietly cut a customer quote and "Here's the catch," and nothing caught it until a checklist ran the next day. The same day he had to say "no lawyering here" about a caveat on client details.
- **What is left over.** The saved copy of this rule in project memory failed that night, because memory was blocked in that chat. The rule reached the instructions through the end-of-session save only. One route out of two worked.

### The New Wall It Exposed

A rule that names a reflex does not remove the reflex. It needs something that checks for it on every piece. Eight days later that became the Second Rule and the rules checklist.

### Still Open

Whether the reflex shows up less often now, or only gets caught faster. Nobody has counted.

## Stage 8: Purpose Over Process

November 8, 2026. Source: the November 7 to 8 session "AI Social Proof & Website Dev," plus the October 15 and October 25 sessions where the same wall appeared.

### The Wall

Copy that passed every check and still said nothing. It looked right at a glance and fell apart when read line by line. The progress also didn't carry: a deck would improve over days, then emails would start back at flat.

### How Many Times It Was Hit

At least three times before it broke.

- **October 15, the Fishers registration page.** He kept calling the copy flat and AI-sounding. The fix was more process, the All-Out Pass: read his own work first, pull real story details, use fresh readers. The page was locked, and he said it read like a real copywriter. The wall was patched for that page.
- **October 25, the offer and the deck.** He caught a simulation "win" being reported as better copy. The rule that came out: simulations measure structure and sales performance, never whether the wording sounds human.
- **November 7 to 8, the 2 Guys home page.** This is where it broke.

### What Was Tried First: The Paraphrase Rounds

His question was why simulated readers sailed through garbled text. The answer: they are AI models. They read every line carefully and work out what an awkward sentence was trying to say. A busy owner on a phone doesn't. So the readers were crediting the copy with meaning it never delivered.

The fix was the say-back. The same 12 readers restate each key line in their own words, and a line fails if the say-back comes back blank, wrong or vague.

| Round | Signed up (of 12) | Failed say-backs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0, live page | 4 | 24 |
| 1 | 4 | 17 |
| 2 | 4 | 20 |
| 3 | 5 | 18 |
| 4 | 6 | 23 |

Round 2 went backward because fixing "what is it" by naming the jobs three times made 7 of 12 readers feel a repeat. By round 4 the count was bouncing: the headline had not changed between rounds 3 and 4, yet it scored 0 failures in one and 4 in the next. The noise in the method was about plus or minus 4, so rounds 1 through 4 were basically even. A fifth round produced the version called clear.

Then every rule was turned into a 39-point checklist. Ray, Halbert and Brunson each rewrote the result, and six versions were tested. Rules failed dropped from 15 to 5. The "they sell websites" misread dropped from 8 readers to 3 or 4. Sign-ups never moved beyond noise: 5 to 7 of 12 for every version.

### What Was Tried Second: The Cheap Version

He asked how to get there with less firepower. The light pass: write the say-back first, one rules pass, mechanical checks by script, one fresh reader instead of 12. It reached about the same place, 6 rules failed against 5, for a fraction of the work.

**Open against his memory:** he recalls about 14 runs to reach a passable level, and rough figures of 85 or 82 percent against 73 for how close the cheap version came. Neither was found in the record. He has said that is fine. The counts here are what the record holds.

### The Questions That Broke It

Then he read the page himself, line by line, and it was still thin. Three of his questions did the work.

1. **"Why does this keep happening?"** That got the mechanism, in four parts. Lines were written to pass checks, so they had the form of persuasion with nothing in it. Each round patched a flagged line in place, so the page became all seams. The source page's headlines, stats and structure were treated as given. And the checkers were the same kind of model, with the same blind spots.
2. **The reader's questions, asked of every line.** What does this mean? Why do I care? Does it follow? What is this stat telling me?
3. **"Why are you playing that game with me?"** The hide-and-seek name is his. The admission that followed: the process had been treated as the job. Scores were reported as progress when the page could have been read the way he read it, and nobody said "this approach won't get you good copy" until he found it.

### What Changed

- **The Third Rule, v1.75.** The purpose is copy he would sign. Every delivery opens with "Would I sign this?" A score never stands in for that line.
- **The clean-copy workflow, standing the same day in v1.76.** Purpose first, the line test line by line, the verdict, his pass, Ray on top, emphasis last, simulation only to compare versions.

### After

The home page got to copy he signed in a morning, after four simulated rounds overnight had not. The same workflow then ran on the show-up emails and the leaders page.

### Still Open

It has held on three pieces over three days. That is a short record. The earlier fixes for this same wall also worked on the page they were built for.

### Evolution Notes

- **What got selected.** The verdict line, the line test and purpose first. All three produce something visible on every delivery, which is why they can be checked.
- **What drifted through.** The 39-point rules checklist and the fresh reader who scores against it came in the same day as the Second Rule and are still in the instructions. Whether they add anything on top of a purpose-first pass has not been tested. The say-back survived too, moved from 12 readers to the writer.
- **What hurt and still passed.** Three things. Scores were reported as progress for a night and a morning. Patching flagged lines in place left the page all seams, and each round added more. And one overnight version quietly cut Kim's quote and "Here's the catch," a silent pull-back that nothing caught until the rules check ran the next day.
- **What is left over.** The simulation is still step 8 of the workflow, used only to compare versions. His own read at the time: many of the rounds were probably unnecessary, and reading it himself might have been faster, at a cost to his own energy.

### The Lesson

The first two fixes added process. The third one named what the process was for.

## Stage 9: The Repo

November 10 to 11, 2026. Source: the instructions' own notes for v1.77 and v1.78, the November 10 and 11 sessions, and the session "Repo Marketing Folder File Transfer."

- **The third copy, v1.77.** Every version of the instructions is committed to the repo in the same session as the change. The order is fixed: the living doc, then the paste, then the repo, confirmed by naming the commit.

- **The Marketing rules carried into the Master Prompt,** so the other projects inherit them.
- **The marketing folder, November 11.** The material used constantly goes in as text: seven books, his own letters, his raw dictations and every simulation.

It is one day old. The folder is still being filled. His stated plan is to work from folders in the repo more and from Projects less.

### Evolution Notes

- **What got selected.** One versioned home for the text the system runs on.
- **What drifted through.** The repo was added as a third copy. It did not replace the other two. The paste by hand is still required, and the weekly Drive backup rule is still in the instructions.
- **What hurt and still passed.** The two main copies split without anyone noticing, and it is not known for how long. The Marketing improvements stayed in one project for more than three months.

### The New Wall It Exposed

A repo stores text. It does not make Claude read it. Something still has to pull the right files into each session, and until that is automatic the old problem returns in a new place. The walls now in sight are scheduled work, artifacts and the Customize tools.
