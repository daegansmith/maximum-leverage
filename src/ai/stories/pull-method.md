# I Gave My AI My Own Sales Letters. It Still Wrote Like Everybody Else.

You hand your AI everything you've got.

Your best work. Your notes. Your research.

And you figure, now it knows me.

That's what I figured too.

[[1]]

Late July. I set up a project in Claude just for my marketing.

Then I loaded it.

My own sales letters. The notes from my own masterclass. Research on 16 copywriters.

Five days later, August 1, I asked for a page.

A landing page to sell the Invisible Funnel. That one is mine. I built it.

[[2]]

We got a headline. Right sentiment. Moving in the right direction.

Then came the line under it.

[[3]]

Claude wrote:

"I made more money by refusing to sell than I did in twelve years of trying to."

Try after try like that.

[[4]]

So I asked Claude the question I'd ask about anybody's copy.

"Would you opt in? Would you wanna read more? I wouldn't."

And Claude said:

"Honest answer: no, I wouldn't either."

Later I said it flat.

"What you have is nonsense. We are... really got a lot a lot to learn."

I ended up writing the line myself.

"How I made more in one weekend than I ever have in any given year of owning my business..."

But that left a question I couldn't let go of.

It HAD my letters. So why didn't it sound like them?

[[5]]

We went looking.

My instructions said protect the voice. Claude was checking that box once a session.

Once. Not before every draft.

**It HAD my letters. It wasn't reading them when it wrote.**

That's the lesson.

**Loading your AI with your work is NOT the same as your AI using it.**

[[6]]

That night a rule went in. Check the knowledge base before every draft. No "I already read it earlier this session."

And I told Claude why.

"I don't want you to ever go to default mode and just create some generic boring nonsense."

Did it hold?

No. The same wall came back October 15. That one is Stage 4.

So here's what it boils down to.

1. Load your work. That's the easy part.

2. **Make it go back to your work before every draft.**

3. Then ask it what it read.

But before that wall came back, I hit a different one.

For seven weeks, my AI was reading [an old version of me](../one-long-prompt/). And nothing told me.
