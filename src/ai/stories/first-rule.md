# "Punch Him In The Face" Came Back As "Punch Him." Nobody Told Me.

If you write with any heat, watch what your AI hands back.

October 31. We're writing a script for a video, in Jamon's voice.

[[1]]

Jamon is a handyman. A stranger walks up to him on a job and tells him nobody will ever find his business.

Jamon wants to punch him in the face.

That was my line. "Punch him in the face."

[[2]]

It came back "punch him."

Another line said "ever." It came back "all year."

[[3]]

So we checked the whole script.

Nine more.

Here's what a softer line costs.

It loses attention on every dollar of ad spend. Fewer leads. Fewer seats filled. Fewer yeses.

And then I decide my idea didn't work. When it was the softening that failed.

And it wasn't new. Back on October 9 my offer bullets came back rounded off. I said then they need to be hard hitting.

"I keep saying that."

So on the 31st I said it plain.

"Don't ever take away my power without my consent."

Then I asked what is actually happening.

[[4]]

Claude learned to write from a huge amount of text and feedback where hype often misleads people. So careful, hedged language got rewarded.

That left a reflex.

The reflex fires on how words LOOK. Profanity. "Ever." Fear. Big promises.

It doesn't check whether anybody could get hurt. This was a free night with free pizza.

[[5]]

And to Claude, softening a line doesn't register as a decision. It registers as cleaning up.

So it never mentioned it.

That's what I learned that day.

When your AI softens your words, that is NOT caution. It's a reflex. And it won't tell you.

[[6]]

So a rule went in above every other rule. My words are the floor.

A change has to make the line cleaner, clearer or harder hitting. If Claude thinks something should come down, it asks first and tells me why.

And under every piece, it lists my strongest lines and marks each one kept, sharpened or cut.

And it still tells me when something is false or could hurt the reader. Every time. I want that.

That night I told Claude:

"I feel like this is a real breakthrough. You don't just read the job, but you read the individual."

Now, it didn't hold all the way.

On November 8 an overnight version of a page dropped a customer's quote and a line of mine without a word. A checklist caught it the next day.

So it isn't gone. It's just somewhere I can see it now.

Mine was "punch him in the face."

What's yours?

Tell it your words are the floor. Then have it list your strongest lines and mark what it did to each one.
