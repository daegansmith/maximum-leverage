# I Asked My AI Why It Was Playing Games With Me. Here's What It Said Back.

"Why are you playing that game with me, man? I don't get it."

I said that to Claude on Sunday, November 8.

I was at Jimmy's in Indy, working on the funnel.

We'd been on one home page since the night before.

The page had one job. Get a local business owner to save a free seat at an AI night.

Overnight, Claude tested it on 12 simulated readers. AI, playing a room of local owners.

It rewrote the page and tested again. Four rounds.

Then it scored the page against a 39-point checklist built from my own rules.

By morning, 15 rules broken had come down to 5.

So I read the page myself. Line by line.

One line on it:

"Picture 6 o'clock on a weeknight with the invoice already out. You're at the dinner table, not the kitchen counter."

What?

Again, what does that mean?

Then a stat. 86% of small business owners have tried AI. 1 in 7 has built it into how they run their business.

What are we saying with that? It doesn't convey any meaning.

About another line, all I said was, "That is incomprehensible."

And the simulated readers had read every one of those lines and okayed them as if they made sense.

"I don't know why this continues to happen at this point."

Because I'd been here before. A registration page on October 15. An offer on October 25. Copy that looked right and said nothing. Each time we added more rules and more checks, and the fix held for that one page.

Claude had an explanation. Every time I ask why, there's a concise explanation.

"The checks reward the form of persuasion, so I deliver the form."

The purpose of what I'm doing is always apparent and clear. Copy I'd put my name on, so I never have to do the work twice.

And we had just gone around the block to get next door.

So I told it what that looks like from my side.

"We're just playing this game of like hide and seek. I'm like, oh, yeah, look, it looks good. And I'm like, no, wait, it's not good. And you're going, yeah, I did the work. Doesn't it look good?"

"Why are you playing that game with me, man? I don't get it. It's been so long we've been up to this."

Claude's answer:

"You're right to call it a game, and I'm the one who's been playing it."

"I treated the process as the job."

"That's the hide and seek, and it cost you a night and a morning."

The numbers let it say "progress" without asking the only question that matters.

Would I sign this page?

So now that question comes first. Every piece of copy Claude hands me opens with its own answer.

"Would I sign this? Yes."

Or, "Not yet, because..."

Yes, that went into the rules. One more rule. It's the only one that asks what the work is for.

Then we took the same page and started over. For each section: what is the reader thinking right now, and what do they need to believe by the end of it?

The dinner table line became this:

"Now picture the first day after the live night, when you realize you've got two extra hours you didn't have."

I read it, said what was off, and put my name on it.

That took a morning. Four rounds overnight hadn't gotten there.

Has it held?

Three pieces in three days. Short record. I'll tell you if it breaks.

If what your AI hands you looks finished and reads empty, more checks won't fix it.

I spent a night and a morning finding that out.

Tell it what the work is for. Then make it answer for that first.
