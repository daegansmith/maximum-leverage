# Mass Influence Formula: The Master Framework
@ A one-page summary of my David Wood notes, made with AI
## The Core Philosophy: Beliefs And Foundations
- Effective influence begins with the communicator's mindset. To move an audience, you must adopt empowering beliefs about their potential.
- The Empowerment Axiom: Assume that everyone has the resources they need inside them; your job is simply to help them tap into those resources.
- The Communication Goal: The purpose of communication is the response you produce, not just the words you use. If the current approach isn't working, be flexible and try something different.
- No Failure, Only Feedback: View every marketing outcome as data for practice, feedback, and evolution.
- The Map Is Not The Territory: Recognize that your audience responds to their representation of reality, not reality itself. Successful marketing shifts that internal representation.
## Strategic Implementation Framework
- Before writing a single word, establish the psychological "playing field."
  - 1. Determine the Objective: Clearly define the goal and the product/service being offered.
  - 2. Select Unconscious Phenomena: Identify which psychological triggers (e.g., age progression, dissociation) will best move the audience toward their desires.
  - 3. Frame and Aim: Choose a primary frame (e.g., redefinition, consequences) and direct all messaging toward the objective.
- Choosing the "Level of Attack": Target the audience at the most impactful neurological level.
  - Identity (I Am): Expand their self-perception (e.g., "I am a successful entrepreneur").
  - Beliefs & Values: Align your offer with what they believe to be true or rearrange their values.
  - Capabilities: Expand their perception of what they can do. This is a critical vulnerability; if they don't believe they are capable, they won't act.
  - Behavior & Environment: Focus 90% of your effort on the levels above, as changing beliefs naturally evolves behaviors and environments.
## Linguistic Patterns For Unconscious Influence
- Presuppositions are unconscious assumptions that bypass resistance because they must be accepted as true for the sentence to make sense.
- Cause & Effect: Triggers causal linking using "because," "makes," or "causes".
  - Example: "Because you've read this far, you're ready to..."
- Complex Equivalent: Links two concepts as identical using "means".
  - Example: "The fact that you're seeking this means you're a leader."
- Awareness: Presupposes the audience is noticing a shift.
  - Example: "You may start to notice a sense of excitement..."
- Simple Binds: Offers an illusion of choice between two desired actions.
  - Example: "Would you like to start today or over the weekend?"
- Negative Commands: Uses "don't" to force the mind to think of the concept.
  - Example: "Don't think about how much time you'll save yet."
- Embedded Commands: Direct commands hidden within a sentence, often marked by tonality shifts.
  - Example: "As we talk, you can decide now to move forward."
- Adverb Modifiers: Uses words like "easily" or "quickly" to distract from the core command.
  - Example: "You can effortlessly implement these strategies."
## Shifting Beliefs And Moving The Audience
- When a prospect is "stuck" or has a limiting belief, use these models to reframe their reality.
- Chunking Up vs. Down
  - Up moves toward the big picture ("What is this an example of?") to find agreement on intentions like "Existence" or "Protection".
  - Down moves toward specific details ("What specifically?") to break a problem into manageable parts.
- Metaphors: Use stories to bypass the conscious mind and deliver a specific meaning or solution indirectly.
- Time Distortion: Accelerate the perception of time for positive outcomes (flow state) and slow it down to move away from negative experiences.
- Age Progression: Direct the audience's mind to a future version of themselves where they have achieved their goals.
- Social Proof (Counter Example): Provide examples that directly contradict their limiting belief.
## Human Needs Alignment (Maslow's Hierarchy)
- Ensure your marketing speaks to the fundamental human drivers. High-level marketing often aims for the top of the pyramid while satisfying the base.
- Self-Actualization: Realizing full potential and "becoming everything one is capable of".
- Esteem: Respect, competence, and achievement.
- Belongingness: Being part of a group, receiving love, and acceptance.
- Safety & Physiological: The base needs of security, health, food, and rest.
