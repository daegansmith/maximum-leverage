Rule #1: Never Guarantee Personal Recruits

This one's huge, I got excited after the success of my first co-op and got a little too ballsy.

I started telling people exactly how many sign ups they'd get from my efforts.

Dumb!

You can't control this part of the process online no matter how good you are and it's totally the wrong expectation to set, so don’t.

Rule #2: Never Let Participants Buy More Than 2 Shares

When I first started my own co-ops I didn't realize just how powerful they actually are, and as a result I had people buying like 10 shares at a time.

Now you may say, that's great, that just more money.

The problem is this creates the wrong team mentality.

When a person tries to buy more than 2 shares from you they’re basically trying to have you finance their business.

Your co-op is there to help people out when they get started it’s NOT there to let lazy people send you money to create teams for them. It’s a hand up, not a hand out.

And from a team sense, what do these people who do nothing but give you money do when people DO join through them? Nothing. Their team dies off.

Everyone loses.

Rule #3: Never Let Anyone Participate In Your Co-op More Than 3 Months In A Row

Again, your co-op is a hand up, not a hand out.

Co-ops are so powerful you really do have to limit how much and how many times people give you money to participate.

If you don't ween them off of your system, so they can go out and get their own, they never will, and that's just not a recipe for fostering leadership or long term success.

Rule #4: Never Let Your Co-op Grow To More Than 100 Participants

The type of co-op I'm going to be teach you run in the "Perpetual Duplication" system is specifically designed for efficiency to so that one person can run it without hassle, and well ...

The honest truth is once you get more than 100 people things start to get really hard to manage.

It's hard to get enough QUALITY traffic within the co-op run of a typical month and as a result you end up bleeding into the next month fulfilling your previous months co-op and then everything gets pushed off schedule.

Our largest co-op was somewhere in the range of 180 people and it just wasn't worth it.

There's definitely a point of diminishing returns and it's right around the 100 member mark.

Stick with that number, write it down, and you'll be just fine.

Besides, once you get to 100 people participating in your co-op you'll likely have about 10 good leaders (The Rule Of 10% Right?) to pass the process off to, so it'll also be the point where you know it's time to develop the next generation to take over your leadership role, so it works kinda perfect.

Once you get 100 co-op participants it’s a sign for you to pass on the reigns to the next generation.

From there you get to step back and watch things continue to thrive.
