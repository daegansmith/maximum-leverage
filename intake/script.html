Client Intake · The Night

The Consultation Script

The hour on Zoom, after the owner has walked through every step. Modeled on the HyperResponsive List call.

Draft, October 20, 2026. For Jimmy, Daegan, or a hired closer on Zoom with an owner who has walked through the whole intake. E60 is the example.

An outline with anchor lines, not a script to read. The anchor lines carry the wording that matters. Say everything around them in your own words.

Where She Is When The Call Starts

She’s seen her video, three looks of E60, and the readout.

She texted SHOW ME and got all eight fixes, built out.

She’s answered three questions, met Jamon and the owners Jimmy works with, done the homework, and signed the agreement.

Nobody has asked her for a dime.

She isn’t wondering who you are.

She’s wondering whether you see E60 the way she does.

That’s the call: show her you do, then show her the way out.

Your Frame

You’re the doctor, not the salesman.

She came to you, and you’re deciding whether E60 is a business you can take to its next step.

You don’t need this sale. She can feel it when you do.

Ask, then be quiet.

Write down her exact words. You’ll hand them back to her at 0:45.

0:00 The Open

“Erin, good to finally meet you.”

“How are you feeling going into tonight?”

Whatever she says, ask what’s behind it, then reassure it.

“We’re here to finish E60’s site, and to figure out the right next step for the business.”

“Sound good?”

Get the yes.

0:01 The Homework Check

“Did you get through the homework?”

“What was new for you, and what just confirmed what you already knew?”

Let her answer. It shows you whether she watched, and it puts her a little on the spot.

If she skipped it: “No problem. Let’s find a night this week once you’ve watched it. It’ll make this hour twice as useful.” (Q3 is still open: move the night, or hold it.)

0:03 Why I Do These Myself

Ask first: “Can I tell you why I do these calls myself?”

Keep it under a minute.

“We only take a few businesses at a time, and every one becomes a story we tell, like Jamon’s.”

“So I look for owners where I can see the next step, and I know how to get them there.”

“If that’s you, and you’re up for making the changes, I’ll say yes with open arms.”

“Either way, you leave tonight with the diagnosis.”

“Fair?”

Then: “And when I ask you something, don’t hold back.”

0:05 The Recap: What Did We Miss?

The diagnosis is already done. You’re not discovering. You’re confirming, and letting her talk.

Put the readout on screen.

“Here’s what a stranger sees when they find E60.”

“And here’s what your members say it feels like.”

“Two different places. Here are the eight things we’d change.”

Then hand it to her: “What did we miss?”

This is the longest stretch of her talking. Write down her emotional words.

Then, in your own words:

  • “What made you text SHOW ME? What was going on that day?”
  • “How’d you end up owning a gym?” (Find the common thread: family, the work, money, what she does for fun.)
  • “What have you tried that didn’t work the way you hoped?” (These become the symptoms at 0:45.)

0:15 What She Hates, And Where It’s Heading

“What do you do all day that you hate doing?”

“And what does it cost you? Not money. What does it cost you?”

“Where’s the gap between where E60 is and where you want it?”

“If nothing changes, where is E60 a year from now?”

That last answer is the left road at 0:48. Write it down.

Give her a real tip or two as she talks. Tell her what she’s doing right. Set her straight where she’s off.

0:25 The Build

Build the rest of her site live, in her words. Do the research on screen as part of the build, so she’s watching her own business change.

Start with her answer from Step 6: “When does a new member become one of yours?”

Read it back to her, then put it on the page.

Every time you use one of her lines, say so: “That’s yours. You said that.”

0:40 What Still Excites Her

“You’re almost done. I’ve got a pretty clear picture. One more thing I’ve been wondering.”

“In all of it, what still gets you excited about this place?”

0:42 Three Years Out

“Picture E60 three years from now, doing better than you’ve let yourself imagine.”

“You’re walking in on a Monday morning.”

“What do you see?”

If she drifts into her life outside the gym, let her for a moment, then bring her back: “And inside the building?”

Don’t fill the silence.

0:45 What I Really Get About You

“Here’s what I get about you, Erin.”

Read back three or four things she said, in her words.

Then the turn:

“Everything you’ve tried, the ads, the posts, the discounts, was treating a symptom.”

“That’s not on you. You were doing what everyone tells gym owners to do.”

“Here’s the root cause.”

Then one sentence, from the readout, as an analogy. Draft for E60:

“E60 is a great team playing without jerseys.”

“Your members know exactly who you are. Nobody walking by can tell.”

“Fix that one thing, and the rest gets a lot easier.”

0:48 The Crossroads: The Four Ways

The phone script’s crossroads is the four ways. (Daegan is reworking this screen.)

Put it on screen.

“There are four ways to get all eight done. I’ll be straight with you about each one.”

  • Leave it. Nothing changes. Hand her back her own answer: where E60 is a year from now.
  • Do it yourself. All of it is doable, and here’s what it takes. And who runs the gym meanwhile?
  • Hire it out. The agency numbers she’s already seen. And she manages every one of them.
  • With us. Easy on her. All eight, live in weeks.

“Looking at all four, which one’s right for E60?”

Wait. Let her pick her square.

0:50 Is It A Must?

“I’d love to have E60.”

“Can I ask you one thing first?”

“Is this a must for you right now, or a someday?”

Let her answer.

Then why her, specifically. Pick the two that are true:

  • “You’ve already got the hard part: members who love the place.”
  • “You’re in momentum. The outside just has to match the inside.”
  • “You did the homework. Most owners don’t.”

0:52 What That Looks Like, And The Price

“Want the short version of what working with us looks like?”

Put the offer on screen. One sentence per piece, each tied to something she said tonight:

  • Your site, built, hosted and run. (The site you just watched me build.)
  • The follow-up that turns a free week into a membership.
  • The front desk, off the phone. (The thing you said you hate.)
  • Members coming back, on repeat.
  • A meeting with me every month, to steer it.

“It’s $1,997 a month.”

“No setup fee.”

Then stop talking.

Don’t justify the price. Wait.

When she speaks, or when it’s been long enough:

“So what are you thinking? Excited? Nervous? Or are you like, Jimmy, stop talking, let’s go?”

0:54 She’s In

Take the card on the call (Version B), or drop the link in the chat (Version A). The money question is still open.

Celebrate while you do it.

Tell her exactly what happens next: “First thing I build is your free-week page. It’s live by Friday.”

The heads-up: “Two weeks in, you might wonder if you did the right thing. Most owners do.”

“When that happens, text me, and I’ll show you where we are.”

End on a high: “Welcome. This is going to be fun.”

If She Needs Time

[OPEN: a deposit to hold her spot, the Secret Sauce move. Only if it’s real for this offer.]

If She Says No

Thank her, and mean it. She keeps the diagnosis and the readout.

“If something changes, text me.”

Then the follow-up takes over. [OPEN: the sequence for owners who don’t buy on the night.]

Open

  • The root cause line at 0:45, per business, from the readout. The jersey analogy is a draft.
  • Any limit on how many businesses Jimmy takes, only if it’s real.
  • The deposit, and the follow-up for a no.
