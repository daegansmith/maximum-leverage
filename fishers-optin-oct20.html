The Morning A Stranger Walked Out Barefoot And Called His Baby Ugly

Jamon was down on one knee in the clay, building a gazebo.

Busiest he'd ever been.

Still barely keeping the lights on.

The back door slapped open.

Out came a guy with no shoes and hair going six directions.

He didn't say hello.

He pointed at the truck.

"T-J-O-A-T? Nobody's ever gonna find you."

Jamon's jaw locked up.

Hands at his sides, in fists.

That barefoot stranger was me.

Ten minutes later I came back out with a laptop.

On the screen was his business.

Same name. Same work.

Except now it looked like the company you'd call if your own house needed fixing.

He read it twice.

Then I showed him one more thing.

GottaCallJack.com.

All they gotta remember is they gotta call Jack.

He waived his fee for the whole job, right there.

About twelve thousand dollars.

Two months later he was on the radio, with a jingle and a TV spot running in Indianapolis.

Then he had to turn the ads off.

Not because they weren't working.

Because the crew couldn't keep up.

"Remember when I couldn't grow because there was no time? Now there's no time because we're growing."
