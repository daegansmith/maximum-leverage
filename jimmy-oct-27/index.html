Jim, Today At Launch Fishers

Jim texted that he's ready and has a lot of ideas for AI. Treat today as the first audit.

How Jimmy Runs It

  • Record it. Let Jim talk for the first twenty minutes and get everything out.
  • Ask the questions that find the money: budget, priorities, what tools and systems he uses, his real numbers.
  • Don't sell today. Make it about everything that's possible.
  • Close the meeting on a promise, not a price.

"Let's figure out what you need today. I'll go put the pieces together, and by tomorrow I'll have a plan for you.

Then we'll go through it, what you want to do and what you don't."

The second meeting, later this week, is the plan with options: if you want this, it's this much. If you want that, it's that much. Daegan's read: Jim won't say no, he'll pick A, B or C.

On A Percentage Deal

Jimmy floated it from stage, and Jim said he'd be open to it. Only with an upfront fee.

Why: we don't know who hosts his site, how he runs his sales, or what the real numbers are. A pure percentage puts all the risk on us in a business we haven't seen inside yet.

Where Daegan Would Start With Jim

  • His story first. It's the most powerful part of the equation.
  • Explainer videos, and a site overhaul built around them.
  • Sell more to every customer: simple pricing changes, and subscriptions instead of buying once and coming back.
  • Then scope the rest.

Dr. McCray: The Second Kind Of Proof

Doc is a numbers guy. He'd been tracking six months of everything in one spreadsheet: money in and out each day, insurance, conversion, shows and no-shows.

Jimmy screen recorded Doc walking through it on his Mac, sent the video and the spreadsheet to Claude, and got logins to his practice software and his Google account for reviews. A lot is already automated.

That gives the presentation range:

  • Jamon: we made him more money and gave him a new image.
  • Doc: we automated so much that the business runs without him.
  • A whole other type of business, which tells the room this works for theirs too.

Blur anything private. Screenshots, the workflows and the numbers go in the deck.

Doc may be at the event on the 9th. Jimmy is confirming today.

The Belief: A Door To A Whole New World

Jimmy's been chasing a feeling he couldn't get into words. He found it in the shower, as an analogy:

Oil is stored energy. Old dinosaurs, baked into the ground.

Put that energy into machines, and for the first time machine labor replaces human labor. That's the Industrial Revolution.

Now imagine you just discovered gas, and you're only starting to play with it. That's AI. We're in the first inning, and this one will make the Industrial Revolution look quaint.

Most of the room only knows AI from the news, where it's bad. What they've seen isn't even one percent of what it can do.

The job isn't to teach it. It's to make them see it as magic, then: imagine we unleash this at your place.

The look on his face when he had the bracelet is the feeling. It's cool, but it's really a key, a door to a whole new world.

Jimmy pictures it as a short video that tells the story and ends with: here's where we are now. Daegan already knows where it slots into the deck.

Where The Presentation Stands

After going through Allie Miller's work, Daegan rebuilt it around demonstration and simplified a lot. Much clearer.

Then the simulations: a cold room of Fishers and Indianapolis owners, some invited by Jimmy and Paul, some not, running one version against another.

  • The latest versions: 15 of 24 bought, and 13 of 24.
  • Before the fixes it hovered around 40 percent.
  • The biggest blind spot: saying big numbers turned the room off. They got reframed.

It's close to peak. What's left is believability and the strongest social proof:

  • Jimmy's story, pulled from his past presentations.
  • How Paul is included.
  • The client proof, starting with Doc.

The Offer: What The Full Build Really Costs

Jimmy's worry: the top tiers are full builds. With Jason building the systems, he'd sell it all day. Without that, how much work is a full build?

Nobody knows yet. That's the point of running one or two real clients through it.

  • Find what people are actually buying on. The simulations say some components can go.
  • Daegan built the best offer he could on his own. Jimmy is in front of these owners; he'll know what to cut, simplify, and where his story fits.
  • That needs two or three hours together, with the offer and the delivery side by side.

How Every Client Goes: The Baseline

The offer is together. How we deliver it is still a black box. Here's the starting path, for us to agree on:

  1. They buy and get a confirmation email.
  2. We collect their basics.
  3. We send their first mockup, the way Jimmy does now.
  4. They pick a direction.
  5. They fill out an automated, in-depth marketing questionnaire.
  6. We turn their answers into line items: where they're leaking, where we need more information, where they're making good money and could make more.
  7. The meeting fills the gaps.
  8. We agree on the scope for that client.
  9. The build starts.

Run a couple of clients through it, then make it better: cut, add, move, or combine.
