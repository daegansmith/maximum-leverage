Stage 6 · October 26, 2026

The RAM And The Split

The Wall

Drift. Going through the Fishers deck on October 25 and 26, rules agreed early in the session stopped holding later in the same session. That included rules already written in the instructions. He was correcting the same things twice in one sitting.

Terms used here, defined in the Glossary: drift, a tool call, the RAM, the governing docs, the Doctrine, the Changelog, the copy load.

  • How It Was Hit
    • The slide read-through. Rules set at the start of the session were being broken a few batches later.
    • The first fix failed on its first use. A short document of the rules in force was created. The rules were written into it. Then the next batch went out without anyone reading it.
    • His words: "the whole purpose of it being created was for it to be read before every then following response." And on having to catch it himself: "that's super draining."
  • What Broke It

    Seeing that two different actions had been treated as one. Writing a rule into a document and reading that document are separate acts. Only the reading changes what comes out next. And a reading that leaves no trace cannot be told apart from no reading.

    So the read became a tool call: a visible step, made last, right before the work is built. It either shows on screen or it did not happen.

  • What Changed
    • The RAM, v1.67. One document per job, one screen long at most, holding the rules in force right now. It is pulled from the instructions when the job opens and grows as he names rules or repeats a correction. When it outgrows a screen, something leaves. The first one, for the Fishers deck, held seven rules.
    • Only the read counts. If the read is not visible, the batch was not checked.
    • The split, v1.68. That same day the instructions were measured: about 14,400 words. Roughly 46 percent was craft rules by format, 10 percent was version history, and only about a third was the operating rules that govern a turn. Three kinds of document were sitting in one file. They became three: the instructions (about 6,400 words), the Doctrine and the Changelog.
    • First or last? He asked whether the RAM should be read first or last. Last. Everything else is where the content comes from; the RAM is the final check before anything leaves.
  • After

    The trial note written that evening: the read fired on every batch of the next read-through, and drift inside the session stopped. Two weeks on, the RAM for the leaders page (November 10) lists fourteen rules in force.

  • Evolution Notes
    • What got selected. The visible read. It is the first fix in this log that goes after the cause found at Stage 4, that a written rule does not cause its action. The one-screen limit was selected with it.
    • What drifted through. The RAM is still listed as On Trial in v1.78. Its decision date was the end of the Fishers deck, which has passed. It is the most relied-on part of the system and has never been formally kept. Also carried: a to-do from that night, to move about 2,400 more words out of the instructions into skills, with no record yet of it being done.
    • What hurt and still passed. The first version, a rules document nobody read, lasted one use. The larger one: the RAM stopped drift inside a job, and it did not stop rules being skipped when work was framed as a test or a quick rewrite. Thirteen days later the Second Rule had to say that tests and experiments are copy jobs too.
    • What is left over. The long instructions are still read in full, by rule, alongside the RAM. And the instructions have started growing again: three new top-level rules were added between October 31 and November 8.
  • The New Wall It Exposed

    The RAM lives inside one project and one job. He said the next day that it should be written into his other projects. That needs a place where a lesson learned once can reach everywhere, which is what Cross-Project Learnings and then the repo are for.

  • Still Open

    Whether fourteen rules still fits one screen. And the verdict on the trial itself.

From the record: the session "Editing slides, Adding RAM, Updating Instructions." Instructions v1.67, v1.68 and v1.70.
