How the whole system works

One rulebook seeds every project. The living documents sit in Claude Docs, and Drive is the filing cabinet. Every business moves through two projects, with you in the middle.

The structure

Every project gets its rules from the Master Prompt, but none of them can see it. So each one carries its own copy, pasted into its instructions. The last line of every copy says which version it is. If that line falls behind, the next session says so.

Once a prompt is out in its own project, it stops being a child and becomes a peer. The projects don't read each other's prompts. They connect through two shared documents that all four read live and write back to at the end of a session: the Voice Reference, which is how you sound and the picks you've made, and Cross-Project Learnings, which is what one project learned that the others can use. Every project can also read the Master itself.

There is one direct line between projects: Research hands Marketing a copy brief. Nothing else passes project to project except through the shared docs.

Where things live

Anything that changes as we work is a Claude Doc. One doc per subject, edited in place, with its version and changelog inside. Every earlier state stays in its history.

Drive holds what doesn't change: source files, references, the old numbered versions, and the weekly backups.

How a business moves through it

Your review is the step that can't be skipped. Everything after it depends on what you correct and add there. The E60 page only worked because three years inside that room went into the brief.

The weekly rhythm

All week, the prompts grow. Every real session ends with quick checks: what worked, what broke, whether the Voice Reference needs a new pick, and whether anything applies beyond this project. Each answer can add a rule.

Once a week, two things happen together. First the backup: you export each living doc to Google Docs, and Claude files the copies in Archive and checks them. Then the refine: cut the repetition out of the four prompts without losing a rule.

Then it grows again. Each week ends a little smarter and no fatter.

What keeps it all working

None of these show up in the pictures.

  • Find by rule. No prompt names a version number. Living docs are found by their title, Drive files by "the newest in this folder." Updating a document never breaks anything that points at it.
  • The version line. Every prompt ends with the version it reflects, so a stale copy can't hide.
  • Never let context go unused. Anything you've shared, a story, a detail, a decision, has to show up in the work. Missing it counts as a failure, not a slip.
  • The ambiguity pass. In Marketing, every line on a page gets read as the reader before the page is handed over.
  • One correction fixes the whole class. A note on one line gets applied to every line like it before anything comes back.
  • A standing to-do. Marketing and Research each keep one, read at session start, so nothing depends on you remembering where you left off.

The idea underneath it all

Every business talks from the position it earned. Every buyer listens from the position they're stuck in. The thing a business is proudest of is almost always the proof of the thing its buyers fear. And nobody has drawn the line between them.

Bread argued nutrition; the objection was taste. A gym argued components; the objection was experience. A contractor argued competence; the objection was fear of the process.
