Stage 7 · October 31, 2026

The First Rule

The Wall

His lines came back weaker than he gave them, and nobody told him. Strong words were swapped for careful ones as if that were tidying up.

Terms used here, defined in the Glossary: the First Rule, the intensity check, the Assumptions Gate, the simulation, the provenance line.

  • How Many Times It Was Hit
    • October 9, the offer bullets. His lines were rounded into plain English and the force went out of them. His words then: these things need to be hard hitting, "I keep saying that." So it was already a repeat by October 9. It was treated as a note on one set of bullets.
    • October 31, the registration video script. Written in Jamon's voice from his dictation. "Ever" had become "all year." "Punch him in the face" had become "punch him." When the whole script was checked for the same thing, there were nine more places.
    • The same session, a second form of it. Claude wanted to check every line with Jamon before writing, as part of the Assumptions Gate. He had already said it was fine.
  • The Questions That Broke It
    1. His rule, stated flat: "don't ever take away my power without my consent." Clean the words up, make him sound better, never weaker. If a pull-back seems right, ask.
    2. His question, asked without wanting a list of what was done: what is actually happening, and how do we work together given it?

    The answer was a mechanism. Claude learned to write from a very large amount of text and feedback in which hype often misleads people, so measured, hedged language was rewarded. That left a reflex. It fires on how words look (profanity, "ever," fear, big promises) and not on whether anyone could be hurt. It does not check the context: a free night with free pizza, where the worst outcome for a viewer is a good evening. And from the inside it does not register as a decision. It registers as cleaning up, which is why it was never reported.

    The damage was not the caution. It was that the editing was silent. He could only fight what he could see.

  • What Changed
    • The First Rule, v1.71, placed above every other rule. His words are the floor. A change has to make the line cleaner, clearer or harder-hitting. If it is softer, it does not go in.
    • No silent pull-backs. If something should come down, Claude asks first, with the reason. A softer version can sit beside his, labeled, never in its place.
    • The intensity check. Under the copy, his strongest phrases from the source, each marked kept, sharpened or cut. A softened line has nowhere to hide.
    • Where the line is. Claude still calls out anything false, unethical or harmful to the reader, every time. Where nobody is harmed, he gets the rein.
    • Build first, confirm at the junction. Creative work does not stop mid-draft to ask permission. The person it touches reads it afterward and says yes or changes it.
    • His amendment the same day, v1.72. He did not want a rule that only protected his version. If Claude thinks its words would do better, it makes the case and both go to a simulated cold crowd, then a live split test. His phrase: "I want everyone to win."
    • Who he is, v1.72. The first passage in the instructions that describes the person and not a procedure: he thinks to the outcome, he will not sell what he believes could hurt people, and why he once stopped marketing.
  • After

    His words that night: "I feel like this is a real breakthrough. You don't just read the job, but you read the individual." He marked it as a new day for how the work is done.

  • Evolution Notes
    • What got selected. The floor, the ask-first rule and the intensity check. The intensity check works the way the RAM does: it turns something invisible into something that shows on the page.
    • What drifted through. The virtual test for disagreements. It was written in on October 31, and no record has been found yet of Claude making a case against one of his lines and testing it. The rule may be doing its work by existing, or it may be unused.
    • What hurt and still passed. The softening ran for at least three weeks after he first said "I keep saying that." And the rule did not end it. On November 8 an overnight version of the home page quietly cut a customer quote and "Here's the catch," and nothing caught it until a checklist ran the next day. The same day he had to say "no lawyering here" about a caveat on client details.
    • What is left over. The saved copy of this rule in project memory failed that night, because memory was blocked in that chat. The rule reached the instructions through the end-of-session save only. One route out of two worked.
  • The New Wall It Exposed

    A rule that names a reflex does not remove the reflex. It needs something that checks for it on every piece. Eight days later that became the Second Rule and the rules checklist.

  • Still Open

    Whether the reflex shows up less often now, or only gets caught faster. Nobody has counted.
