The Path, Step By Step

The real drawing. Red is what gets the yes to the next step.

A free 15-minute call.

A second free call, with one real fix.

Then the first money: a paid assessment, $999.

Then a build, $1,000 to $10,000 and up.

Or a monthly plan, $1,000 to $2,000 a month.

Or both.

Doing it yourself is free, and it stops there.

What Gets Each Yes

The only ask is the free call.

He listens.

He doesn't pitch.

Call two is booked before he hangs up.

By the time he asks for money, he's already handed over a real fix, free.

Then one question: do it yourself, or want my help?

Free means you do it yourself.

The review ends on: "Want me to build the first one?"

Then two calls a month, one thing built on every call.

How He Keeps Them, And How He Prices

A running list of everything he's built.

That list is why they stay.

Anything not on it is a new invoice.

Fixed fee, never hourly.

Estimate the hours, add 20%, times his rate, round it.

What I'm Taking From It

Once I could see it, I could judge it.

For us, the best first yes is simple: tell us one thing, we just listen, and we see if we can figure it out.

Zero risk.

If you're interested in AI, why wouldn't you say yes to that?

So for us:

Two calls first.

A free seat at the next night comes with them.

The paid assessment is collected at the end of call two, while we're on the call.

The monthly plan comes at the end of the assessment review.

It's vastly different from how we do it now.

And it asks less of us.

Two places still have to be made irresistible:

The offer after we show them the one fix: do it yourself, or want help?

And the offer at the end of the review: most clients want us to build at least a couple of things. Which ones?

That's the next thing we build.
