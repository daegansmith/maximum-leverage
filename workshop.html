The Workshop

How To Take Your Business Places It's Never Been…

The opening, the story, and the logical chain of beliefs.

An evening with a handful of local business owners. It isn't a class. It's an argument built one belief at a time, and every belief gets demonstrated on a real business in the room rather than claimed.

Before the first belief comes the opening and a three-minute story. One line runs through the whole night: AI plus the right marketing is maximum leverage, and maximum leverage equals freedom. Six lessons get written down along the way and come back as one slide at the end.

After each belief lands, a new objection shows up. Those are written below in the audience's own words, because an objection is the signal that the next belief hasn't been earned yet.

Approved slides are shown under each section.

Belief 1B

The Basics Are Simple, And You're Learning Them Right Now

The objection gets said out loud before anyone can sit with it privately, then answered by handing over the real basics live.

The Five Ground Rules, Then The Room Tries Them

  • Tell it who it is. Give it a job before you give it a question.
  • Give it context: who you are, what you sell, who your customers are.
  • Say exactly what you want back. Ten options, ranked, beats "some ideas."
  • Don't take the first answer. The second and third are almost always better.
  • Use the right model for the job.
  • Then the room does it: the question they asked earlier, asked again with all five rules on the fastest model, then again on a different model.
  • Me too: the same question three ways on screen, asked bare, then with the rules on model one and model two. Nobody has to explain the difference.

The Modeling Principle

  • Want to do something you don't know how to do? Find someone who does it well and copy how they do it. They'll watch that happen all night.

Not Rocket Science

  • Did any of that go over your head? Nobody says yes.
  • Then the yes: you can do this, right? The room says it before the plant.

The Plant

  • Said once, honestly, and not defended: even with the rules, their output won't match his. Twenty years of knowing which questions to ask is what goes in.
  • The tool amplifies what you bring. Anybody can draw: same pencil, different hand. A stick figure next to the Mona Lisa.

The wall this raises

The home stretch.

Same Tool, Different Results

  • AI is compressed knowledge, every book on every shelf in one phone, and it makes whoever's using it a genius at what they already do well. But it needs a lens: you.
  • Same AI, person A, person B: an apple and an orange. Results will vary drastically.
  • Here's what I mean… Jamon's estimate: one job, one price. A marketer looked at it and got the idea right away: good, better, best at $5,000, $7,000, $10,500.
  • Good, better, best = more money. The proof: Dan Ariely's Economist test, $8,012 per 100 readers with two options and $11,444 with three, 43% more. What nobody realizes… good, better, best = more customers, because the question shifts from "yes or no?" to "which one?"
  • Exercise: now it's your turn. The room creates their own good, better, best, and two or three share.
  • The formula, filled in: AI plus a contractor is one price and one customer; AI plus a marketer is more per customer and more customers. Same tool. Different person. Different result.

The Equation

  • So here's the equation… curiosity, purpose, refining, result. The room did every one today.
  • How to use AI for Maximum Leverage: ask, run the loop, keep the one that works, ask again. Maximum money, minimum time.

Belief 8

Three Ways Forward

Belief 7 ends on "Three choices."

The roads follow directly, each shown with a doodle that carries its trade-off, and the trade-offs are spoken, not written.

Road One: Do It Yourself

  • Here's what I did; go do it, if you want to. It takes time and experience, and it's a process. But it's fun, and you'll do it for the rest of your life.

Road Two: Have Someone Do It All

  • A smart choice as a business owner: you don't have to worry about it, and the money comes in. But you're separated from the process and you don't control it, and it's the most expensive option. It depends on the type of person you are.

Road Three: Do It Together

  • With someone who knows how. That's why I'm here. The only risk is that you do some of the work, and that's the best price to pay: you take ownership, it's yours, and you're free to part ways whenever you want.
  • Naming a downside for your own option is deliberate: when the person making the offer names its cost, the room trusts that person more, and trusts the comparison.
