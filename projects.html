The Mission

The Mission Behind Maximum Leverage

It isn't AI. It's leverage.

Everything else on this page is a part. This is why the parts exist.

Right now, the presentation proves an owner needs us.

That sells a service.

Daegan wants more than that: "this isn't just something that we're selling, some commodity, some service. No, we ourselves are a vehicle for transformation."

That's the layer this adds, to everything we make.

Why It Matters: Money

Daegan's reason is "purely for money."

When an owner can see the mission, they stop shopping for a service.

They're joining something. And that's worth more.

His read on Russell Brunson: "one funnel away" wasn't the magic.

The mission behind it was.

And it grew every time he told the story.

The Mission, As It Stands

Seven pieces, in Daegan's words.

  • Leverage is the core. AI is "a commodity at this point."
  • The lessons that built it, told as parables. Traffic. The list. The free hotel seminar where a man nobody knew told one story, and a hundred people walked to the back of the room. About $100,000 of business, that day.
  • The lesson under all of them. "If you tell the right story to the right audience, you can never go broke."
  • The fall and the return. "I did give up once. And I don't want that to happen for you anymore. Because I did, and it's not a good place to go. And I'm back."
  • AI is one more lesson. "It's just a new variable." What it does is let everything he learned work for anyone's business.
  • The thought we want every owner to have. "He did it for himself and he taught people. But now he can just do it for me."
  • What it turns into. "Your business ain't a business, it's a pathway to the lifestyle you want."

Still open: our version of "one funnel away." The one line. It comes out of the stories, not before them.

What The Mission Does To The Owner Across The Table

Every owner walks in thinking the same thing.

"I need a new website."

Logic answers that. A better site, a better price.

That's a service, and anybody can sell it.

The mission answers something else. Watch what happens in their head when they hear the stories:

  • "I need a new website." Where they start.
  • "Wait. These guys are different." The first story lands.
  • "This isn't about a website." The story was about their life, not their site.
  • "Because of AI, they can do something nobody else can." He did it for himself. Now he can do it for them.
  • "They'll make me more money, and they'll keep me from losing it." Two stories, side by side.
  • "I need them." Not the website. Us.

Why Only We Can Get Them There

60 years of experience between the three of us.

A guy who just popped up out of nowhere with AI can build a site.

He can't tell the story of a weekend that made $350,000, or of walking away from it, or of coming back.

Anybody can tell an owner how AI will change their business.

We sell who's looking at the business.

AI is how fast we can look. What we see comes from who's looking.

The Research Tool

This is how we look at a business and see what it's missing.

You give it a business. Usually just the website address.

It reads that business the way a customer would. Then it hands back four things:

That last one matters most.

A finding is nice. A finding with a dollar figure on it is a reason to act.

How A Run Works

Before it opens a single page of the business, it does three things. The order is the whole point.

  1. It reads what it already knows. The standing to-do, Cross-Project Learnings, and the Voice Reference. So the run starts with your judgment loaded, not generic instinct.
  2. It writes down what a buyer in this category needs to hear. Eight to twelve questions, before it looks at the site. Once you've read a page, you can only see what's on it. The list is what lets the run notice what isn't.
  3. It reads every review as one dataset. Not as praise. As evidence of what customers cared about, what they feared before buying, and the words they use. This is the most valuable step in the run.
  4. Only then does it open the site. Every page in the navigation and footer, read against the list. Each question lands as answered well, answered badly, or not answered at all.

Your firsthand knowledge comes in at the review step, not the start. It deepens findings that already exist. It doesn't steer the research before it's looked at anything.

How A Business Moves Through It

Your review is the step that can't be skipped. Everything after it depends on what you correct and add there. The E60 page only worked because three years inside that room went into the brief.

Where It Plugs In

We're still working out exactly where it meets the funnel. For now, two places are clear:

  • When someone registers. By then we know something about their business: the name, the industry, maybe the website.
  • When we get a client. It's where every build starts.

Once we know where it fits, it goes into the picture at the top of the page.
